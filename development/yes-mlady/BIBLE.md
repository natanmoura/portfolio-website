# Yes M'lady — the bible

Canon. Everything else defers to this document.

---

## 1. The premise in one paragraph

The world is broken and stratified. Men, monsters and a few things that are not alive at all pass from hand to hand under an old compulsion. You take one in. He serves you, badly, in the manner of his nature. When you are ready to be rid of him, you name **one thing** you would have changed. He is compelled to accept. He says *"Yes, m'lady,"* and he goes, carrying your change and your note to whoever finds him next. He will try to be what you asked. He will probably get it wrong. The record of every attempt is stapled to him forever.

---

## 2. The title is the mechanic

**"Yes, m'lady."** Always those four words. Always the comma.

It is the sound of the compulsion closing. Even a man who despised his keeping is bound to give her that one moment of reverence on the way out, and the binding does not care whether he means it.

It is also, obviously, the other thing. The phrase arrives on the card the first time within the first sixty seconds of play, and it sets the register permanently: obedience that is not quite obedience, deference with something underneath it, a service posture that the server did not entirely choose and does not entirely resent.

**Design rule: the words never change. The delivery always does.**

The card renders the line and then, beneath it, how he said it, drawn from his current state:

> *"Yes, m'lady."*
> He said it the way a man agrees to be hanged.

> *"Yes, m'lady."*
> Too fast. He had been waiting to be asked for something.

> *"Yes, m'lady."*
> Through his teeth, and then again, softer, as though the first one had not counted.

> *"Yes, m'lady."*
> He said it to the floor. The floor has heard it before.

Every delivery is logged in his ledger forever. They are the most collectible objects in the game and they cost nothing to produce.

## 3. The thesis

**Eroticism is imagination, and imagination needs a gap to work in.**

So the game withholds. It suggests, it implies, it stops one clause early. It is not coy about desire and it is not explicit about anatomy. When it goes direct, it goes direct about *wanting*, never about mechanics. Innuendo carries the load. The reader finishes every sentence herself and that act of finishing is the product.

**Eroticism is vulnerability, and vulnerability requires flaw.**

This is the real bet. Every character is broken in a specific, named, permanent way. The game does not hide the flaws, it publishes them. It publishes the failures, the rejections, the tally of women who took one look and let him go the same night.

And the wager is that attraction grows in exactly that exposure. That the man with forty rejections and a long humiliating ledger becomes, at some point, the one somebody takes in **for the story**, and then somebody else does, and then he has a following. That the time a player spends understanding a flaw is the time in which she becomes attached to it.

**The trojan horse: the game looks like it is about collecting handsome men. It is about learning to want the damage.**

---

## 4. The world

Late medieval, poor, damp, unjust. Not a fairy tale. Not grimdark either, because grimdark is humourless and this is often very funny.

**Station is real and it bites.** A knight and a ratcatcher are not the same kind of problem. The game does not pretend class is decoration. A highborn's flaws are expensive and other people pay for them. A lowborn's flaws are cheap and he pays for them himself. Editing a man's station is possible and it is one of the cruellest things you can do, because station is the one thing that changes everyone else's behaviour toward him without changing him at all.

**The compulsion is old and nobody knows who laid it.** There is no wizard to petition, no plot to break the curse. It is weather. The characters have theories. Most of the theories are wrong and a few are heartbreaking.

**Everyone here has already lost something.** Tragic backstory is the baseline, not the exception. Some of it is printed on the card. Most of it is not.

## 5. Nature, flaw, and the conservation law

### The six axes

Every character has six values, 0 to 100, and for each one a **nature anchor**: the value his nature pulls back toward when nobody is watching.

| Axis | 0 | 100 |
|---|---|---|
| **Warmth** | cold | tender |
| **Restraint** | ravenous | disciplined |
| **Station** | gutter | crown |
| **Beastliness** | mannered | monstrous |
| **Candour** | veiled | naked |
| **Devotion** | free | bound |

Current values drift under edits. Anchors barely move, and only over very long lineages. **The gap between current and anchor is the strain**, and high strain shows up in his behaviour as visible effort, which is one of the most attractive things in the game.

### The flaw is conserved

**A flaw can be transmuted. It can never be deleted.**

This is the single most important rule in the system. Every character has one named, permanent defect. An edit aimed at a flaw does not remove it. It **redirects** it into a new shape that carries the same weight.

> Brother Anselm confesses everything to everyone. A keeper edits: *be less forthcoming.*
> He stops confessing aloud. He starts writing it down. He leaves the pages where she will find them.
> The flaw is intact. The flaw has learned a new trick.

Over twenty keepers, a flaw becomes a baroque and specific and deeply strange thing, and its history is legible in the ledger. **This is where personality actually comes from.** Not the profile. The scar tissue.

### Resistance and warped compliance

Each archetype has a resistance value per axis. Push against nature and the edit is unlikely to take cleanly.

**There is never a refusal. There is always a strange yes.**

Three outcomes:

- **Took.** The change lands. Least interesting. Should be roughly 35% of edits.
- **Warped.** He tried, in his own grammar, and produced a third thing that is neither what he was nor what she asked. Roughly 50%. **This is the game.**
- **Backfired.** The effort to comply made the original trait worse. Roughly 15%. The most shared outcome.

The warp is never random. Each archetype has a documented characteristic warp per axis, written so that the failure **reveals the nature more clearly than compliance would have.**

> Lord Orsolt is told to be warmer.
> He warms his hands at the fire for a full hour before he touches her. The hands are the right temperature. Nothing else about him is. He is very pleased with himself.

> Ser Aldous is told to stop kneeling.
> He stops kneeling. He now stands too close and too still, at attention, waiting for an order that is not coming, and it is somehow worse, and she cannot look at him.

**Write the warps like jokes with a knife in them.** The laugh and the ache in the same line.

## 6. The loop

### Encounter
A card surfaces from the pool. Face, name, station, two or three lines of the profile, the flaw stated plainly, and the ledger summary: kept how many times, released how many times, longest keeping, times passed over. Refusing costs nothing except that it goes in his record, and he is told.

### Accept
The full profile opens. More lore. Whatever previous keepers unlocked and chose to leave visible. The last note. The last edit and how it went.

### Keeping
He does one thing per day, in character, shaped by his current axes and his accumulated warps. She responds. A thumb, essentially, though it will not look like a thumb.

The daily deed is the retention loop and the primary content cost. It is also the mechanism by which **locked backstory unlocks**: certain reactions, certain streaks, certain days of the moon crack something open, and a piece of him arrives that is not on the card and that most keepers never see. Unlocked lore is hers to publish to the card or keep private, and that choice is itself a small moral event.

### Release
1. She declares she is ready.
2. She names the one change.
3. He resists, visibly, for a beat. Everyone gets this beat, including the mirror.
4. *"Yes, m'lady."* Plus the delivery line.
5. She composes the note.
6. He goes.

The edit resolves **after** he leaves. She never sees whether it took. The next keeper does, and it is in the ledger, and she can look him up later, and that ache is deliberate.

## 7. The Almshouse

The mechanic that makes the thesis true instead of merely stated.

Characters passed over enough times fall out of general circulation and into **the Almshouse**, which is a public room, browsable by anyone, where the least wanted are displayed with their full humiliating ledgers.

Taking one out of the Almshouse is marked permanently on both of you. It grants visibility, a badge, and a place in his record: *"Taken from the Almshouse by ——, on the ——, after 41 refusals."*

This is where the community darlings will come from. A character with a long enough record of rejection becomes a folk figure, and the first woman to take him in becomes part of his story, and other women will take him in **to be part of it too.** The Almshouse converts failure into narrative gravity.

Leaderboards must therefore never rank on retention alone. Retention selects for pleasant. Rank on volatility, on divisiveness, on most-transmuted flaw, on Almshouse rescues, on longest lineage.

## 8. Voice and register

**Do.** Suggest. Understate. Stop one clause early. Use hands, throat, the back of the neck, wrists, the line of a jaw, the space between two people, doorways, the temperature of things. Let the reader do the work. Be funny. Be sincere about strange desires, because sincerity is what makes strangeness erotic instead of a punchline.

**Do not.** Anatomy. Explicit acts. Crassness, unless crassness is that specific character's whole problem, in which case it is characterisation and it should still cost him something. Winking at the audience. Treating the objects and monsters as a joke.

**Punctuation.** No em dashes. No semicolons. Periods and commas.

**Length.** Card copy is short. Two or three lines. The lore behind the card can breathe, but the card itself must be readable in the time it takes to decide.

---

## 9. Non-negotiables

1. The phrase never changes. Only the delivery.
2. One edit per keeper. No exceptions, no purchases, no premium second edit. The scarcity is the game.
3. Flaws are conserved. Nothing is ever fixed, only rerouted.
4. No refusals. Only strange compliance.
5. No free text anywhere a stranger will read it. See `GRAMMAR.md`.
6. Every word of invented vocabulary has a glossary entry with an author and a date. See `LEXICON.md`.
7. Suggestion over explicitness, always, with rare and deliberate exceptions that land harder for being rare.
8. Objects and monsters are played with total sincerity. The Harness is not a bit.
9. He advances one phase at a time, only on a held signal, never on silence. Pushiness is frequency and eloquence of asking, never a shortcut past the answer. See `MECHANICS.md` section 3.
10. His history is veiled until she takes him in, and it opens at the speed of understanding him. See `LOOP.md` section 4.
11. Three verbs, every day, forever. Yes, No, Say nothing. Anything richer is a rare offer, never a standing menu. See `MECHANICS.md` section 2.
12. **Loyalty is never rewarded and breadth is never taxed.** No decay, no neglect penalty, no jealousy stat. The game exists so she can explore widely and find out what she wants, and any rule that narrows the roster argues against the whole product. See `LOOP.md` section 6.
13. No numbers on the card. Numbers live on the ledger, one tap away, for whoever wants them.
