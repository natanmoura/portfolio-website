# Yes M'lady — the grammars

Two composers. The edit, which changes him. The note, which changes what the next woman expects. Neither has a text field.

---

## 1. The edit composer

She gets one. It is the whole game and it should feel like it weighs something. Slow the interface down here. This is the one screen that must never feel efficient.

### The forms

Six sentence shapes. The first is the workhorse and covers most edits. The other five are rarer, cost more, and hit harder.

**1. The dial.** Common.
> Be ⟨more | less⟩ ⟨TRAIT⟩ ⟨when | about | toward⟩ ⟨CONTEXT⟩.

*Be less courtly about the understair. Be more beast-patient toward strangers.*

Two slots and a hinge. The `CONTEXT` clause is what stops the system being a stat slider, because it makes every edit situational and therefore interpretable, and interpretation is where the warp lives.

**2. The prohibition.** Uncommon.
> Never ⟨VERB⟩ ⟨OBJECT | PLACE | TIME⟩ again.

*Never count the barley again. Never kneel in the chapel again.*

Prohibitions aimed at a flaw are the most reliable way to transmute it, and transmutation is permanent. Players will learn this and it will become the advanced move.

**3. The lesson.** Uncommon.
> Learn to ⟨VERB⟩ ⟨OBJECT⟩ ⟨TONE⟩.

*Learn to hold a glove without being asked. Learn to lie beautifully.*

Teaching a nature something outside itself has the highest warp rate in the game. Roughly seventy percent. Almost all of the best failures come from here.

**4. The unburdening.** Rare.
> Give up ⟨OBJECT⟩.

*Give up the gorget. Give up the ledger. Give up the ninth stair.*

Taking a thing away from a character who is partly made of that thing. Very cruel. Very good.

**5. The disclosure.** Rare. Unlocks only if she found something.
> Tell the next one about ⟨SECRET⟩.

The only edit that moves lore instead of stats. She takes something he told her in confidence and compels him to keep telling it. This is a genuine betrayal and the game should let her feel it, because some of them are so much better once the thing is out.

**6. The station.** Very rare, gated, expensive.
> Be ⟨raised | lowered⟩ to ⟨STATION⟩.

The only edit that changes nothing about him and everything about how the world treats him. Almost always cruel in both directions. Reserve it, make it hard to reach, and never let it be undone in one move.

### The flinch

After she confirms, before the words, he resists. Visibly. Everyone gets one, including the Mirror, including the Harness.

The flinch is rendered from his current axes and his relationship to the specific edit. A `Devotion` 90 knight barely flinches and that is its own kind of sad. A fae flinches for a long time and then smiles, which means she has made an error somewhere in the wording.

Then:

> **"Yes, m'lady."**

And the delivery line beneath it, drawn from state. See `BIBLE.md` section 2.

### Resolution timing

The edit resolves after he is gone. **She never learns whether it took.** The next keeper does. She can look him up months later and read what her change became in somebody else's hands, and that delay is the most emotionally loaded mechanic in the design. Do not shorten it to make the loop tidier.

## 2. The note composer

One note. Written to a stranger. Composed from templates and slots.

### Constraints

Between one and three lines. Two is the sweet spot and the interface should nudge toward it. Long notes are not read and short ones cut deeper.

Every note is signed with her handle and dated, and shows how long she kept him. **The note is never anonymous.** Anonymity is what makes these spaces rot. A name attached to a note, with a visible keeping history behind it, does most of the social regulation for free, and it lets reputations form, which players want.

### The templates

Grouped by what the note is actually for. She picks a shape first, then fills it, and the shape is itself expressive.

**Warnings**
> Do not ⟨VERB⟩ him. He will ⟨DEED⟩.
> He will not ⟨VERB⟩. Stop asking. I asked.
> Whoever taught him to ⟨VERB⟩ should be ⟨FAILING⟩.
> Mind the ⟨OBJECT⟩.

**Instructions**
> Ask him about ⟨OBJECT | PLACE⟩ and then ⟨VERB⟩.
> Keep him past ⟨TIME⟩. That is when ⟨DEED⟩.
> Leave the ⟨OBJECT⟩ where he can see it.
> Do not thank him. I thanked him.

**Verdicts**
> He is ⟨TRAIT⟩, and worse, he knows it.
> ⟨TRAIT⟩, but ⟨TRAIT⟩.
> I kept him for ⟨DEED⟩. I let him go for ⟨DEED⟩.
> I wanted ⟨TRAIT⟩. I got ⟨DEED⟩. I am not sorry.

**Confessions**
> I did not ⟨VERB⟩ him. I should have.
> The ⟨PART⟩. That is all I will say.
> I only meant to keep him ⟨TIME⟩.
> He was ⟨TRAIT⟩ and I was ⟨TRAIT⟩ and neither of us mentioned it.

**Bequests**, unlocked by long keepings only
> I am sorry about the ⟨OBJECT⟩.
> Whatever you do, he is ⟨TRAIT⟩. Lamplit. Take him.
> He came to me ⟨TRAIT⟩. He leaves ⟨TRAIT⟩. Both were mine.

### Worked examples

Everything below is assembled from the banks. Nothing was typed.

> *I kept him for the doorframe hours. I let him go for the counting.*
> — Ilse of Marrow Row, kept 19 days

> *He is thrall-warm, and worse, he knows it.*
> — Beatrix Sarn, kept 2 days

> *Ask him about the barley and then leave the room. Trust me.*
> — Ilse of Marrow Row, kept 19 days

> *Do not thank him. I thanked him.*
> — Anonymous is not permitted here, and this note is worse for the rule, and the rule stays.

> *He will not kneel. Stop asking. I asked.*
> — Marta Vosk, kept 41 days, kept-past-nine

> *Wrists. That is all I will say.*
> — Yesenia of the Low Field, kept 6 days

> *He came to me hollow. He leaves scold-warm. Both were mine.*
> — Marta Vosk, kept 41 days

The last one is what a great note looks like, and it is entirely constructed, and it will make somebody take a suit of empty armour into her house.

### Reading notes

A character carries his **last three notes** on the open card. His full note history lives in the ledger and is browsable, and it is the best reading in the game. Notes can be seconded rather than replied to, which surfaces the true ones without creating a comment thread. There are no comment threads anywhere in this product.

## 3. Safety, without a moderator

The whole system produces zero moderatable strings by construction. Every published word came from a bank the house wrote. The residual risks are three and all are addressable in design.

**Combinations the house did not foresee.** Some pairing of slots will be genuinely vile in a way no individual entry is. Mitigation: an offline pass over the high-frequency combination space before launch, and a blocklist of pairings rather than words. This is a finite, checkable set, unlike open text.

**Targeted use.** Notes are about characters, never about keepers. No template accepts a handle. No slot contains a second person reference aimed outward. Enforce structurally.

**The queue.** Coinage proposals are the only free text in the product, they are never published unreviewed, and they are attached to a handle. See `LEXICON.md` section 7.

And borrow the move that made Kind Words work: a **signed compact at first launch**, in world, stating what this place is for. Roughly three percent of their volume ever flags, and most of that is off-topic rather than abusive. Framing does more moderation than filtering ever will.
