# Yes M'lady — the core loop

Supersedes the day-clock unlock table in `MECHANICS.md` section 1. Everything else there stands.

The governing correction: **time does not open a man. Understanding does.**

---

## 1. The two tracks

The most important structural decision in the game. Two separate meters that players will assume are one, and are not.

**Phase, 0 to 5.** The consent ladder. Physical and declared closeness. She controls the pace entirely and it can move fast if she wants it to.

**Regard, 0 to 100.** How much he will tell her. Earned only by meeting his needs, capped daily, and it does not care what phase you are at.

**You can reach the latch in four days and still be holding a stranger.**

That is the sharpest thing this game has to say, and the writing must land it rather than assert it:

> He stayed. He told me nothing. I had assumed those were the same door.

Phase without Regard produces its own distinct content: presence, compliance, warmth, and a locked room behind it. Regard without Phase produces the opposite and is equally rich, and several of the best keepings in this game will never leave phase 2.

Neither track is the correct one. That is the point.

---

## 2. The Need

Every character has one **Need**, native to his nature, and it is almost never the obvious thing.

| | Need |
|---|---|
| **Ser Aldous** | To be dismissed. Not loved. Released. He needs someone to tell him to rise, to go, to stop. |
| **Wick** | To be caught, and not punished. Witness without consequence. |
| **Lord Orsolt** | To be allowed to finish. Patience with the count, not rescue from it. |
| **Bran** | To not be asked. Especially in moonlast, especially when she badly wants to. |
| **Ilyr** | To be in debt. Refusing his gifts feeds him. Accepting them starves him. |
| **Brother Anselm** | To be interrupted. The absolution he will not give himself. |
| **Corwin** | To be told the truth about the hour. Every time. Knowing he will lose it. |
| **The Harness** | To be given an order it can actually obey. |
| **The Ninth Stair** | To be walked on deliberately. |
| **The Ashcombe Mirror** | To be looked into on purpose. |
| **Hob** | To be needed without being thanked. |
| **Prince Ceril** | To give something that costs him. |

Half of these are met by **withholding**, and that inversion is the whole skill of the game. The woman who understands Aldous does less for him, not more.

### The tell

Every daily deed carries a **tell**, a small signal of what he needs today. Not labelled. Written into the deed.

> He knelt to bank the fire and then stayed down, arranging coals that did not need arranging.

The tell is *stay down*. He is asking to be dismissed and cannot say so. **No** or **Wait** answers it. **Yes** rewards the kneeling and he sinks a little further, and Regard does not move, and she will not know why for a week.

Reading tells is the daily skill and it is genuinely learnable. Right verb, Regard up. Wrong verb, flat, and he closes by a degree that is visible in tomorrow's deed.

The tells are written in cant, which is how the lexicon earns its keep.

## 3. The hesitation

At low Regard he does not refuse to tell her things. **He deflects, visibly, and the deflection has the shape of the thing inside it.** This is the writing rule that makes secrets feel real rather than gated.

> I asked him about the barley. He said it was a household matter. He said it again, differently, as though the second version were an answer.

> He began to tell me and then found something to do with his hands.

> He said his mother was well. His mother has been dead for sixty years and he did not correct himself, and I did not either.

Every deflection must let her see that there is a specific thing being withheld. Never a blank. Never "he does not respond." **A withheld secret is more erotic than a told one, and the game should be in no hurry.**

## 4. Regard tiers

| Regard | Tier | What opens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 19 | **Wary** | Facts only. Deflections, well written. |
| 20 to 39 | **Civil** | His last note. Oblique references to previous keepers. |
| 40 to 59 | **Open** | His last edit, and what it did to him, in his own words. |
| 60 to 79 | **Confiding** | The first true locked fragment. |
| 80 to 99 | **Naked** | The lineage, the full note history, and he narrates parts of it. |
| 100 | **Lamplit** | The bottom of him. Rare. Months. Permanently credited on his card. |

**Lamplit** is already the highest praise in the lexicon, and reaching it is the single greatest achievement available to a keeper. Most characters will never reach it with most women. The card should say how many times it has ever happened, and with whom.

**Daily cap on Regard.** Two or three points a day maximum, earned in one ninety-second session. So a completionist misses nothing by playing briefly, and nobody can rush a man in an afternoon. Pacing comes from rhythm, not from empty waiting.

## 5. The day, step by step

The whole loop, as played. Ninety seconds.

1. **Open.** Every man in the house has a deed waiting. Never a timer, never "come back in four hours," never energy.
2. **Read the deed.** Two or three lines. It carries the tell and reflects his current phase, axes, curses and fortunes.
3. **Tap one of three.** **Yes. No. Say nothing.** That is the whole interface and it never changes.
4. **See the consequence.** One line. **Never a number.** Regard is shown as his manner changing, and a correct read gets an unmistakable reaction so she learns by feel rather than guessing blind.

   > You said nothing.
   >
   > He rose. He looked at you for slightly too long, and then he thanked the floor.

5. **Next man.** Repeat, or stop whenever she likes.
6. **Occasionally, something else.** One of the rare five is offered. A gift arrives unprompted. A fortune strikes. A fragment cracks open. These are the days people screenshot.

Twenty seconds for a house of one. Three minutes for a house of ten.

### Numbers

**The card never shows a meter.** No Regard bar, no axis bars, nothing quantified anywhere she is looking at his face.

**The ledger, one tap away, shows everything.** Regard, all six axes with anchors, phase, curses, fortune history, tier thresholds. Players who want to optimise get a full instrument panel. Players who want a person never see a digit.

This split is not a compromise, it is the correct answer. Numbers on a face turn a man into a lock. Numbers on a ledger are just a household record, which is exactly what this world would keep.

**Waiting days are content.** When she chooses Wait, tomorrow's deed is specifically about having been left alone. There is no dead air anywhere in this design, and if a day ever feels empty that is a content bug, not a pacing feature.

## 6. The house

**One to ten men. She is never told she has too many.**

Breadth is the point of this game. Exploring a wide roster is how a woman finds out what she actually wants, and any mechanic that taxes it would be arguing against the entire experiment. **Loyalty is an anti-pattern here and the design must never reward it.**

So there is no penalty. There is a cost, and the cost is entirely hers.

### The only real cost is attention

Every man in the house produces a deed every day. She answers as many as she likes. **Regard moves only on the ones she answers, and it does not decay on the ones she does not.**

That is the whole system. No decay, no guilt timer, no neglect penalty, no rule anywhere that punishes a full house. A woman with ten men and twenty minutes a week will have ten shallow keepings, and a woman with two men and the same twenty minutes will have two deep ones, and **both are correct ways to play.**

**half-kept** is already in the lexicon: *taken in and not attended to, the commonest cruelty here, and the rules do not punish it.* The rules still do not. Physics does. That distinction is the whole of it.

### Roster size is the session-length dial

This is the useful accident. A house of one is a twenty second daily ritual. A house of ten is three minutes of real reading. **The player sets her own session length by choosing how many men she keeps**, which is a far better pacing control than anything the designer could impose, and it is invisible.

### They know, and they each take it differently

The house is not a secret. This is a world where women keep men and everyone involved is aware of the arithmetic. **Jealousy is not a stat.** It is twelve different reactions to the same fact, which is free characterisation and directly serves the experiment, because **how she arranges her house is itself a preference she is revealing.**

- **Ser Aldous** is relieved. Less attention is easier. A crowded house is genuinely good for him and that is quietly devastating.
- **Ilyr** is delighted. A crowded house generates more debt.
- **Lord Orsolt** counts them. Of course he does. He knows the number before she does.
- **Bran** is fine about it, right up until moonlast, when he is asked and cannot lie.
- **Hob** works harder and hides better.
- **Brother Anselm** confesses about them. To them.
- **Prince Ceril** assumes he is the most expensive man in the house and is correct.
- **The Ashcombe Mirror** shows her the others.
- **The Harness** does not react, and three keepers have insisted otherwise.

None of these move a number. All of them are content, and the good ones are the ones that are not jealous.

### House deeds

With two or more, some days produce a deed involving **two of them**, and this is the largest free-content win available in the design. A modest authored pool of interaction beats crossed with the roster generates enormous variety.

> The stair announced Wick at an hour it had no business announcing anyone. Ser Aldous was already standing in the hall. Nobody explained themselves.

> Ilyr paid Wick a compliment. Wick has not put it down since.

**Station is a house fact.** Keeping a prince and a ratcatcher under the same roof is a social event in a world where class bites, and both men know exactly where they stand in the room. This is texture, never a penalty, and it is the most on-theme content the house can produce.

### What actually limits her

Not rules. Three real things:

1. **Supply.** She cannot hold ten of the most wanted men because she cannot get them. The queue and the pool are the only scarcity in the game and they operate on the characters, never on her.
2. **Outcome quality.** A man she barely engaged with produces a boring release. Thin Regard means no lore to publish, a shallow note, and an edit made in ignorance. Breadth self-limits through the quality of what it produces, which is a consequence rather than a punishment.
3. **Her own time**, which she is allowed to spend however she wants.

## 7. The keeping, step by step

1. **Arrive.** Queue, pool, or Almshouse.
2. **Meet.** The veiled card. Face, station, ancestral flaw, bare numbers. Take him or do not.
3. **Claim.** He crosses the threshold. Phase 0. Regard 0. Minimum keeping three days.
4. **Learn the tell.** The first week is reading him wrong and noticing.
5. **The turn.** Somewhere in weeks two to four, something changes him that she did not choose. A fortune, a curse, a fragment opening. See section 7. This is the mid-relationship beat and every keeping should have one.
6. **Depth or distance.** She pushes phase, or regard, or both, or neither. All four are legitimate keepings and the game must not rank them.
7. **The decision.** She is ready.
8. **The edit.** One thing. The flinch. *"Yes, m'lady."* The delivery line.
9. **The note.**
10. **He goes.** Her archive keeps everything she unlocked. The edit resolves out of sight, in someone else's house.

Typical satisfying keeping is two to four weeks. Deep lore is three to six. Lamplit is a season.

## 8. Fortunes

Change that nobody authored and no keeper chose. Cheap to run, infinitely fresh, and it makes the world feel like it is happening rather than waiting.

**The causal rule: a fortune changes his circumstance or his Need. The axes follow.** It never patches traits directly. Ceril suddenly solvent is a different man with a different hunger, and it is not obvious that he is a better one.

**Reversals.** Sudden wealth, sudden ruin, inheritance, seizure, a debt called in. Moves Station hard, and everything downstream reorganises. The most disruptive and the most interesting.

**Weather and season.** Global, gentle, shared. A long wet week where everyone's Restraint slips a little and every keeper in the game notices at once. Cheap community feeling.

**News from offstage.** Someone he knew died. A creditor called. His old house burned. Arrives as a deed, changes the Need for a while, and is often the crack a fragment comes through.

**Blessings.** Rare, temporary, and usually inconvenient.

### Curses

The best of them, and mechanically distinct from everything else.

**A curse suppresses one good quality. It does not remove it. The card says so.**

> **Cursed.** His warmth is bound. It is still in there.

She knows exactly who he is. She watches him be unable to be it. That is very close to the thesis, and it creates a goal, an item sink, and one of the more painful things this game can do to a person.

**Curses are not flaws.** Flaws are native, permanent and conserved. Curses are external, visible, and liftable. Keep the two categories clean or the conservation law goes soft.

An edit made while a quality is bound applies to the man underneath, and only reveals itself when the curse lifts, possibly several keepers later, possibly to a total stranger. That is the cruellest good idea in the document.

## 9. Things

He brings her objects. She spends them.

**Unprompted gifts** are the good ones, and they arrive because of who he is and where his Regard sits. **Asked-for gifts** are reliable, and they cost a point of Regard with most characters, and they *gain* Regard with the ones whose Need runs the other way. Ilyr, Ceril and Hob all want to be asked. Learning which is which teaches the Need system without a tutorial.

**The resources**

- **Tokens.** Small personal objects. Common. The currency of small unlocks and coinage proposals.
- **Coin.** Actual money. Comes from station, and from Wick, differently.
- **Salt.** Folkloric. Thresholds, wards, bindings.
- **Candles.** Each burns one vigil.
- **Keepsakes.** Unique, named, non-fungible, from a specific character at high Regard. Never spent except for one purpose, below. Otherwise collected, displayed, and the truest trophies in the game.

**The sinks**

- **Lift a curse.** Salt, coin, and **a keepsake from a character who has the quality that is bound.** To unbind a warmth you must give up something a warm man gave you. This is the best rule in the economy. It makes the collection interconnected, it gives long-released characters permanent ongoing value, and it costs her something she loves to give somebody else back himself.
- **Vigil.** Burn a candle to hold him one extra day past a queue claim. The only way to fight the queue, and it should be expensive, and it should feel slightly shameful.
- **Coinage proposal.** One token. Light friction on the wrangling queue.
- **Alms.** Spend into the Almshouse to raise a resident's visibility. Pure altruism, publicly credited, no return.
- **Publishing lore, seconding a note, standing him down.** Always free. Nothing that matters morally ever costs a resource.

## 10. What this fixes, and what it does not

**Fixed.** Long dead waits are gone. A deed is always ready, the daily cap is reachable in ninety seconds, and waiting is now a deliberate move with authored consequences rather than an absence of one.

**Fixed.** Lore is no longer a calendar. It is a reading skill, and two women who keep the same man for the same month can come away with entirely different amounts of him.

**Fixed.** Content freshness. Fortunes and curses generate change nobody had to write, forever.

**Still open.** The daily deed pool remains the largest cost in the project, and tells make it harder, because every deed now needs a legible signal buried in it. This is not a reason to cut tells. It is a reason to author fewer characters better.

**Still open.** The failure mode to watch is a keeper who reads tells correctly, races Regard, and treats a person as a lock. Some of that is fine and even true to life. If it becomes the dominant style, the counter is to make the highest tiers require *withholding* and *waiting* rather than correct answers, which most of the Needs already do.
